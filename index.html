This study needs a mouse

The task measures how you move a pointer across a map, so it only works on a computer with a mouse or trackpad. Phones and tablets can't take part.

If you're on a laptop with a touchscreen, try again with your trackpad or mouse active.

Comparing two flood hazard map layouts

You'll see a map of the Lisbon waterfront with several flood zones outlined. For each task, you read the details of the outlined zones and pick the one the question asks for. There are twelve short tasks, split into two halves that use different map layouts. It takes about ten minutes.

What gets recorded

  • Where your pointer moves on the map, and where you click
  • Zooming and panning
  • How long each task takes, and which zone you chose

No keystrokes, no names, no email addresses, no location. Your data is labelled with a random session code only, and is stored on a Supabase database hosted in the EU.

Taking part

Participation is voluntary. You can close the tab at any point and stop, and partial data from an unfinished session is excluded from the analysis.

Questions: contact the researcher.

How the task works

Each task shows a map with six flood zones outlined in blue. Every outlined zone looks the same on purpose, so you can't tell them apart by colour or shape alone.

Each zone has details attached to it: how deep the flooding is, how many buildings are exposed, and how much built-up land it covers. You read those details, compare the outlined zones, and select the one the question asks for.

The question sits at the top of the screen the whole time. Work as quickly and accurately as you can.

The two halves of the study reveal zone details differently. Each half starts with one practice task that doesn't count.
